Emitters

An emitter proposes a batch of candidates each round and may learn from what the archive did with them.

Interface

QualityDiversity.AbstractEmitter — Type
AbstractEmitter

Supertype of emitters, which propose batches of candidate solutions and may learn from how the archive received them.

Required methods

  • ask(rng, emitter, archive)
  • batch_size(emitter)

Optional methods

  • tell!(emitter, xs, evs, results)
  • restarts(emitter)
source
QualityDiversity.ask — Function
ask(rng::AbstractRNG, emitter::AbstractEmitter, archive::AbstractArchive) -> Vector{G}

A batch of batch_size(emitter) candidate genotypes. Required. Every candidate is a new object: the archive stores what it accepts by reference, so an emitter must not hand over a genotype it got from the archive or a buffer it writes to again. test_emitter checks this.

source
QualityDiversity.tell! — Function
tell!(emitter::AbstractEmitter, xs, evs, results)

Report the candidates xs returned by ask, their evaluations evs, and the AddResults from the archive, so the emitter can update its internal state. Optional: the default does nothing, so an emitter without state needs no method. An emitter that keeps state about the batch it asked for may require that tell! follows that ask.

source
QualityDiversity.batch_size — Function
batch_size(emitter::AbstractEmitter)

Number of candidates ask returns per call. Required.

source
QualityDiversity.restarts — Function
restarts(emitter::AbstractEmitter) -> Union{Int,Nothing}

How many times emitter has restarted its search, or nothing for an emitter that never restarts. Optional: the default is nothing. A BanditScheduler with ReselectTerminated reads it after every round.

Variation emitter

QualityDiversity.VariationEmitter — Type
VariationEmitter(op; batch_size)

Emitter that draws parents uniformly from the archive and applies the variation operator op, giving batch_size children. A mutation takes one parent per child and a crossover two, as n_parents(op) says; the parents are drawn with replacement. It keeps no state, so it is the emitter of MAP-Elites.

CMA emitter

QualityDiversity.CMAEmitter — Type
CMAEmitter(dim; sigma0, batch_size, strategy, ranker, selection, restart, x0, lower, upper)
CMAEmitter(x0::AbstractVector; sigma0, ...)

Emitter that searches for solutions with CMA-ES, the covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy, in the sense of CMA-ME (Fontaine et al., 2020): it keeps a Gaussian distribution of solutions, and after every batch it moves the distribution toward the candidates that its ranker places first. The optimizer can be another evolution strategy. Solutions are real vectors of length dim; the genotype type of the archive can be any AbstractVector type that a vector of Float64 converts to.

  • sigma0: the initial step size, the standard deviation of the distribution. It has no default, because it depends on the scale of the solutions.
  • batch_size: the number of candidates per batch, 4 + floor(3 log(dim)) by default (rounded up to an even number for OpenAIES).
  • strategy: the optimizer, a subtype of AbstractEvolutionStrategy or a function with the same arguments as its constructors. It is CMAES by default; SepCMAES and LMMAES scale to a high dimension (LMMAES only works there: it needs at least twice as many parameters as batch_size), and OpenAIES follows a gradient estimate. For an option of a strategy pass a function, such as (dim; kwargs...) -> LMMAES(dim; kwargs..., n_vectors=8).
  • ranker: how the candidates are ordered, TwoStageImprovementRanker by default. Use ImprovementRanker with a ThresholdArchive, ObjectiveRanker for an emitter that only maximizes the fitness, and TwoStageRandomDirectionRanker for one that explores a direction of descriptor space. The emitter keeps its own copy of the ranker.
  • selection: how many of the ranked candidates are recombined, FilterSelection by default or MuSelection.
  • restart: when to start again, NoImprovementRestart by default.
  • x0: the mean of the first distribution. Without it, and after every restart, the mean is a random elite of the archive, so the archive must hold a solution when the emitter first asks. The second form takes dim and x0 from a vector.
  • lower, upper: bounds of the solutions, scalars or vectors. A sample outside is drawn again, and moved inside the bounds after 1000 draws. x0 must lie inside them.

ask draws a batch, and the next call of tell! must be with the results for that batch; a second ask before that is an error. tell! ranks the candidates, adapts the distribution and decides on a restart; the restart itself happens at the following ask, which has the random number generator and the archive to choose the new start from. The emitter has the properties iterations, the number of batches told, and restarts.

Example

julia> using QualityDiversity, QualityDiversity.Benchmarks, Random

julia> prob = Rastrigin(10);

julia> alg = CMAME(prob; dims=(20, 20), sigma0=1.0, num_emitters=3);

julia> result = solve(prob, alg; budget=3_000, rng=Xoshiro(1));

julia> coverage(result.archive) > 0.1
true

Evolution strategies

QualityDiversity.AbstractEvolutionStrategy — Type
AbstractEvolutionStrategy

Supertype of the optimizers inside a CMAEmitter: CMAES, SepCMAES, LMMAES and OpenAIES. A strategy keeps a search distribution over real vectors, draws batches from it, and moves it toward the solutions that the emitter's ranker places first. It maximizes: the ranking passed to update! lists the best solution first.

A strategy has the fields dim, the length of a solution, batch_size, x, a dim × batch_size matrix that holds the last batch, one solution per column, and lower and upper, the bounds of a solution as vectors of length dim, which a CMAEmitter reads to move the elite that a search starts from inside them. It defines

  • StrategyType(dim; sigma0, batch_size, lower, upper): sigma0 is the initial step size, lower and upper are the bounds of a solution, scalars or vectors, and batch_size has a default;
  • reset!(es, x0): restart the search at x0;
  • sample!(rng, es): draw a batch into es.x and return it, drawing a solution again if it falls outside the bounds. After 1000 draws outside, the last one moves to the nearest point inside the bounds: with the mean on the boundary of the box, a draw lands inside with a probability that halves with every dimension. A strategy that moves its mean along the stored steps of its solutions computes the step of a moved solution from where it lies;
  • update!(es, order, parents): adapt to the last batch, where order ranks its solutions from best to worst and the first parents of them are recombined;
  • should_stop(es, keys): whether the search has converged or broken down, given the ranking keys of the last batch from best to worst; see rank_key.
source
QualityDiversity.CMAES — Type
CMAES(dim; sigma0, batch_size=default, lower=-Inf, upper=Inf, rank_one=TextbookRankOne())

Covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy (Hansen and Ostermeier, 2001; Hansen, 2016, "The CMA Evolution Strategy: A Tutorial"), maximizing. It keeps a Gaussian search distribution with mean m, step size σ and covariance C, and is the default optimizer inside a CMAEmitter. rank_one, a RankOneRule, sets the weight of the rank-one covariance term; PyribsCMAES is the variant that reproduces pyribs.

A generation has two calls. sample!(rng, es) draws batch_size solutions, x = m + σ B D z with z standard normal and C = B D² Bᵀ, and leaves them in es.x as the columns of a matrix. After the caller has ranked them, update!(es, order, parents) moves the distribution toward the parents best solutions, the first entries of order. The number of parents may differ from one generation to the next, and none is allowed; the recombination weights log(parents + 1/2) - log(i) are normalized each time.

batch_size defaults to 4 + floor(3 log(dim)). With lower and upper, scalars or vectors, a sample is drawn again until it lies inside; after 1000 draws it moves to the nearest point inside the bounds, and the update uses the step to that point.

should_stop(es, keys) reports the termination criteria of CMA-ES: a covariance matrix with a condition number above 1e14, a distribution that has shrunk below 1e-11, a non-finite state, or keys, the ranking keys from best to worst, being flat: the best and the worst are within 1e-12 of each other. reset!(es, x0) restarts the search at x0.

source
QualityDiversity.PyribsCMAES — Function
PyribsCMAES(dim; sigma0, batch_size=default, lower=-Inf, upper=Inf)

CMAES with the rank-one covariance weight of pyribs, c1² instead of c1; see PyribsRankOne. It exists to reproduce results that were produced with pyribs, such as those of the CMA-MAE paper, where it makes a search explore with rounder distributions. Pass it as strategy=QualityDiversity.PyribsCMAES to a CMAEmitter, CMAME or CMAMAE.

source
QualityDiversity.SepCMAES — Type
SepCMAES(dim; sigma0, batch_size=default, lower=-Inf, upper=Inf)

Separable CMA-ES (Ros and Hansen, 2008, "A Simple Modification in CMA-ES Achieving Linear Time and Space Complexity"), maximizing. It is CMAES with a diagonal covariance matrix: it adapts one variance for each coordinate and no correlations, which costs O(dim) for a generation instead of O(dim²) to O(dim³), and it learns faster in high dimension where there are too few samples to learn a full matrix. The learning rates of the covariance update are those of the reference implementation (pycma), which are larger than those of CMAES because there are only dim variances to learn. The step size is adapted as in CMAES.

The arguments, sample!, update! and should_stop are those of AbstractEvolutionStrategy.

source
QualityDiversity.LMMAES — Type
LMMAES(dim; sigma0, batch_size=default, n_vectors=batch_size, lower=-Inf, upper=Inf)

Limited-memory matrix adaptation evolution strategy (Loshchilov, Glasmachers and Beyer, 2019, "Large Scale Black-Box Optimization by Limited-Memory Matrix Adaptation"), maximizing. For solutions of high dimension it replaces the covariance matrix by n_vectors vectors, which cost O(n_vectors * dim) memory and time for a solution, and it needs no eigendecomposition.

A solution is x = m + σ d, where d is a standard normal vector z that the vectors M_j stretch one after the other, d ← (1 - c_d,j) d + c_d,j M_j (M_jᵀ d). After a generation the vectors and the path of the step size move toward the recombined z of the parents, and the step size follows the cumulative step size rule. The learning rates are c_σ = 2 batch_size / dim, c_d,j = 1 / (1.5^(j-1) dim) and c_c,j = batch_size / (4^(j-1) dim), as in pyribs; n_vectors is at most dim. The solutions of a batch are not mirrored.

A high dimension only

LM-MA-ES is meant for hundreds of parameters or more, where the covariance matrix of CMAES costs too much, and it requires a batch of at most half the dimension, 2 batch_size ≤ dim. Each generation the path of the step size keeps a fraction 1 - c_σ of itself; with a larger batch c_σ exceeds 1, the fraction is negative, the path changes sign every generation and the step size follows noise instead of the search. The constructor therefore throws for such a batch. With the default batch, 4 + floor(3 log(dim)), this means dim ≥ 26. For fewer parameters use CMAES or SepCMAES, or pass a smaller batch_size. pyribs refuses only batch_size > dim.

The arguments, sample!, update! and should_stop are those of AbstractEvolutionStrategy. should_stop also reports a step size below 1e-12.

source
QualityDiversity.OpenAIES — Type
OpenAIES(dim; sigma0, batch_size=default, learning_rate=0.001, beta1=0.9,
         beta2=0.999, epsilon=1e-8, lower=-Inf, upper=Inf)

Natural evolution strategy in the form of Salimans et al. (2017, "Evolution Strategies as a Scalable Alternative to Reinforcement Learning"), maximizing. The search distribution is an isotropic Gaussian of fixed standard deviation sigma0 around a point θ. A batch consists of batch_size / 2 perturbations of θ and their mirror images, θ ± σ₀ ε. Each solution gets its centered rank r in [-1/2, 1/2], the gradient estimate is Σ ε (r₊ - r₋) / (batch_size/2 σ₀), and the Adam optimizer (Kingma and Ba, 2015) with the given hyperparameters moves θ along it.

batch_size is even, and by default the default of CMAES rounded up to an even number. With bounds, a pair of mirrored solutions is drawn again if either lies outside, and moved inside the bounds after 1000 draws, and θ is moved inside the bounds after every step, so that the pairs keep landing inside. Unlike for the covariance matrix strategies, the number of parents of update! has no effect: the ranks of the whole batch make up the gradient. A restart resets θ and the state of Adam. should_stop also reports a last step of θ below 1e-9 times its norm.

The arguments, sample!, update! and should_stop are those of AbstractEvolutionStrategy.

Rankers

QualityDiversity.AbstractRanker — Type
AbstractRanker

Supertype of the rules that order the candidates of a CMAEmitter from best to worst after they have been evaluated and added to the archive. The optimizer moves toward the best ones, so the ranker decides what the emitter looks for.

A ranker defines rank_key(ranker, ev, result), which gives a key for one candidate from its Evaluation and its AddResult. Candidates are ordered by descending key; ties keep their order in the batch. A key is a Float64 or a tuple of numbers that compares lexicographically. A batch is flat, and the search restarts, when the best and the worst key are the same.

A ranker with state, such as a random direction, defines reset_ranker!, which an emitter calls whenever a search starts. An emitter keeps its own copy of its ranker.

The rankers are TwoStageImprovementRanker, ImprovementRanker, ObjectiveRanker, TwoStageObjectiveRanker, RandomDirectionRanker, TwoStageRandomDirectionRanker and AcceptedRandomDirectionRanker.

source
QualityDiversity.rank_key — Function
rank_key(ranker::AbstractRanker, ev::Evaluation, result::AddResult)

The key that orders one candidate: a Float64, or a tuple that compares lexicographically, where a larger key ranks earlier. ev is the evaluation of the candidate and result what the archive made of it. A new ranker defines this method.

source
QualityDiversity.reset_ranker! — Function
reset_ranker!(rng, ranker::AbstractRanker, archive)

Prepare ranker for a new search. An emitter calls it when its first search starts and after every restart. The default does nothing; DirectionRankers draw a new direction.

source
QualityDiversity.ObjectiveRanker — Type
ObjectiveRanker()

Ranks candidates by fitness alone, whatever the archive did with them: the optimizing emitter of CMA-ME. With MuSelection and ConvergenceRestart a CMAEmitter with this ranker is a plain CMA-ES that maximizes the fitness; with the default FilterSelection and NoImprovementRestart it recombines as many of the fittest candidates as the archive accepted and restarts when it accepts none, as in pyribs.

source
QualityDiversity.ImprovementRanker — Type
ImprovementRanker()

Ranks candidates by the improvement of their AddResult, the gain over the threshold of the cell. This is the ranking of CMA-MAE, for use with a ThresholdArchive.

With a DensityArchive, whose improvement is the negated density, it ranks the candidates in the sparsest regions of descriptor space first, the ranking of Density Descent Search (DDS).

For an archive where a new cell reports its fitness as improvement, as the other cell archives do, the improvements of new and old cells are on different scales; TwoStageImprovementRanker is the ranking for those.

source
QualityDiversity.TwoStageImprovementRanker — Type
TwoStageImprovementRanker()

Ranks candidates first by what the archive did with them, a new cell above an improved cell above a rejection, and then by improvement. This is the ranking of the improvement emitter of CMA-ME (Fontaine et al., 2020).

source
QualityDiversity.TwoStageObjectiveRanker — Type
TwoStageObjectiveRanker()

Ranks candidates first by what the archive did with them, as TwoStageImprovementRanker does, and then by fitness. A new cell ranks above an improved cell, whatever the fitness.

source
QualityDiversity.DirectionRanker — Type
DirectionRanker

Supertype of the rankers that order candidates by their descriptor projected on a random direction: RandomDirectionRanker, TwoStageRandomDirectionRanker and AcceptedRandomDirectionRanker. The direction is drawn when a search starts, from a standard normal vector scaled by the extent of the archive along each descriptor dimension, so a search that follows it moves toward one part of descriptor space. The archive must report its extent with descriptor_bounds, and its descriptors must be real vectors.

source
QualityDiversity.RandomDirectionRanker — Type
RandomDirectionRanker()

Ranks candidates by the projection of their descriptor on a random direction, the random direction emitter of CMA-ME (Fontaine et al., 2020). See DirectionRanker. The ranker has the property direction, empty until a search starts.

source
QualityDiversity.TwoStageRandomDirectionRanker — Type
TwoStageRandomDirectionRanker()

Ranks candidates first by what the archive did with them, as TwoStageImprovementRanker does, and then by the projection of their descriptor on a random direction. A new cell ranks above an improved cell whatever the projection; this is the 2rd ranker of pyribs. See DirectionRanker and, for the ranking that the CMA-ME paper describes, AcceptedRandomDirectionRanker.

source
QualityDiversity.AcceptedRandomDirectionRanker — Type
AcceptedRandomDirectionRanker()

Ranks the candidates that the archive accepted, a new cell or an improvement, above those it rejected, and each group by the projection of the descriptor on a random direction. A new cell and an improved cell are not told apart. This is the random direction emitter as Fontaine et al. (2020) describe it, where only the solutions that improve the archive are ranked by their projection; with FilterSelection the recombined candidates are the accepted ones in that order. See DirectionRanker.

Gradient emitters

Emitters for differentiable quality diversity, which step along the gradients of the fitness and the descriptor; see evaluate_gradient. An emitter that needs the gradients at points of its own asks for them before every round.

QualityDiversity.GradientArborescenceEmitter — Type
GradientArborescenceEmitter(dim; num_descriptors, sigma0, optimizer=AdamAscent(),
                            batch_size, strategy, ranker, selection, restart, x0,
                            normalize=true)
GradientArborescenceEmitter(x0::AbstractVector; num_descriptors, sigma0, ...)

Emitter of CMA-MEGA (Fontaine and Nikolaidis, 2021, "Differentiable Quality Diversity"), for solutions that are real vectors of length dim and problems with num_descriptors descriptor dimensions and gradients; see evaluate_gradient.

The emitter keeps a solution point θ. Every round starts with the evaluation of θ together with the gradients of its fitness, ∇f, and of its descriptor components, ∇mⱼ, which are normalized to unit length (unless normalize=false), and θ is offered to the archive. The candidates of the round are the branches of a gradient arborescence: θ + c₀∇f + Σⱼ cⱼ∇mⱼ, with the coefficients c drawn from a search distribution of an evolution strategy, CMA-ES by default. The coefficients may take either sign, as in the paper and pyribs. After the round, the candidates are ranked as in a CMAEmitter, the distribution of the coefficients moves toward the best ones, and θ moves by the optimizer along the step Σᵢ wᵢ (c⁽ⁱ⁾ ⋅ ∇) that recombines the selected candidates with the weights of CMA-ES. A round without selected candidates leaves θ where it is.

  • sigma0: the initial step size of the distribution of the coefficients, which starts at zero. It has no default; the paper uses values from 0.002 to 10.
  • optimizer: how θ moves, AdamAscent (the default) or GradientAscent.
  • batch_size, strategy, ranker, selection, restart: as for CMAEmitter, with the strategy searching the num_descriptors + 1 coefficients. The defaults are those of CMA-ME, as in the paper.
  • x0: the first θ. Without it, and after every restart, θ is a random elite of the archive, and the distribution of the coefficients starts again from zero.

A round evaluates batch_size + 1 solutions. The loop of step! gets the point from ask_gradients, evaluates it with evaluate_gradient_batch, adds it to the archive and hands the gradients to tell_gradients! before it calls ask; tell! must follow that ask. The emitter has the properties iterations and restarts, as a CMAEmitter. Solutions have no bounds: a step along a gradient has no natural way to respect them.

source
QualityDiversity.GradientOperatorEmitter — Type
GradientOperatorEmitter(; sigma0, batch_size=64, normalize=true)

Emitter of OMG-MEGA (Fontaine and Nikolaidis, 2021, "Differentiable Quality Diversity"), MAP-Elites with a gradient step in place of a random mutation. It works on a GradientArchive, which keeps the gradients of every elite. For each candidate it draws an elite θ uniformly over the occupied cells and coefficients c from a normal distribution with mean zero and standard deviation sigma0, takes the absolute value of c₀ so that the step never lowers the fitness to first order, and returns θ + c₀∇f + Σⱼ cⱼ∇mⱼ, with the gradients of the fitness, ∇f, and of the descriptor components, ∇mⱼ, at θ, normalized to unit length unless normalize=false. The paper finds the normalization essential and uses sigma0 from 1 to 10.

sigma0 is the standard deviation of the coefficients, sigma_g in pyribs; QDax takes its square. The solutions must be real vectors.

source

QualityDiversity.GradientOptimizer — Type
GradientOptimizer

Supertype of the rules by which a GradientArborescenceEmitter moves its solution point along the step that a round of candidates found: AdamAscent and GradientAscent. They ascend, since fitness is maximized.

source
QualityDiversity.AdamAscent — Type
AdamAscent(; learning_rate=0.002, beta1=0.9, beta2=0.999, epsilon=1e-8)

Move the solution point with Adam (Kingma and Ba, 2015), as CMA-MEGA (Adam) of Fontaine and Nikolaidis (2021) does: Adam keeps decaying averages of the steps (rate beta1) and of their squares (rate beta2), and moves every entry of the solution by about learning_rate in the direction of the average step, less where the steps disagree. learning_rate = 0.002 is the rate of every experiment of the paper. The update is that of pyribs, with epsilon added to the square root of the second average.

source
QualityDiversity.GradientAscent — Type
GradientAscent(learning_rate)

Move the solution point θ by the step Δ of a round, scaled: θ ← θ + learning_rate * Δ. This is the update of CMA-MEGA in Algorithm 1 of Fontaine and Nikolaidis (2021), who use learning_rate = 1. The size of a move follows the size of Δ, so the rate depends on the problem; AdamAscent depends on it less.

Bandit scheduler

QualityDiversity.BanditScheduler — Type
BanditScheduler(pool; num_active, zeta=0.05, window=50, reselect=ReselectTerminated())

An emitter that holds a pool of emitters and runs num_active of them each round, chosen by a multi-armed bandit (Cully, 2021, "Multi-Emitter MAP-Elites"; pyribs BanditScheduler). Emitters that keep adding solutions to the archive run more often.

pool is a tuple (or a vector) of emitters for the same genotype type. The first round runs the first num_active of them. ask returns the batches of the active emitters one after the other, in the order of the pool, and batch_size is the sum of their batch sizes, so emitters of different batch sizes can share a pool. tell! gives each active emitter its part of the batch and counts, for each emitter, the solutions it produced (selections) and those the archive accepted with NewCell or Improved (successes), over the last window rounds.

Then it chooses the emitters of the next round. reselect says which active emitters leave the active set: ReselectTerminated (the default) or ReselectAll. The free slots go to the inactive emitters, the ones that just left included, with the highest UCB1 score

successes / selections + zeta * sqrt(log(total selections) / selections)

as in Algorithm 1 of the paper. An emitter without selections in the window scores Inf, so every emitter is tried, and a tie goes to the emitter that comes first in the pool. pyribs takes the log of the total successes instead and counts the whole run.

A mutable emitter, such as a CMAEmitter, may appear only once in pool; build a pool of several with a comprehension, not by repeating one instance. tell! must follow the ask that produced the batch. The emitters of the pool must return their batches as a Vector, so device emitters cannot be scheduled.

Example

prob = QualityDiversity.Benchmarks.Rastrigin(10)
archive = GridArchive(prob; dims=(20, 20))
pool = (VariationEmitter(GaussianMutation(0.1); batch_size=36),
        VariationEmitter(IsoLineVariation(0.01, 0.1); batch_size=36),
        (CMAEmitter(10; sigma0=0.5, batch_size=36) for _ in 1:4)...)
alg = QDAlgorithm(archive, (BanditScheduler(pool; num_active=2),))
result = solve(prob, alg; budget=50_000, rng=Xoshiro(1))

QualityDiversity.ReselectRule — Type
ReselectRule

Supertype of the rules that tell a BanditScheduler which active emitters to take out of the active set after a round: ReselectTerminated and ReselectAll. An emitter taken out competes for the free slots with the inactive ones and may be chosen again.

source
QualityDiversity.ReselectTerminated — Type
ReselectTerminated()

Take an active emitter out of the active set when it restarted during the round, as restarts tells, and always if it cannot restart, such as a VariationEmitter. The default of BanditScheduler, as in pyribs and in Cully (2021).

source
QualityDiversity.ReselectAll — Type
ReselectAll()

Take every active emitter out of the active set after each round, so that the bandit chooses all of them again.
